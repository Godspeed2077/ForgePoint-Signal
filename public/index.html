Who puts your job numbers together?

The work is the easy part. It’s the pile at the end.

Receipts in the truck. Hours on a notepad. The bid buried in an email somewhere. Send me the pile, however rough it is — you don’t sort it, you don’t format it, and you don’t type anything in. That’s the part I take.

Randy Rockwell — it’s me, not a software company

What actually happens

You send what you’ve got

However it sits. The estimate, the supply-house invoices or statements, the sub invoices, the time cards or payroll, the card statement. A QuickBooks export, the PDFs out of your email, or photos of paper. No format, no login, nothing to fill in.

I take it from there

Every line comes back entered and coded, in a spreadsheet that’s yours to keep. You don’t type anything in, and if a number looks wrong you tell me and I fix it.

You get the page back

One page per job, estimated against actual, by cost code. I tell you the day it’s coming back, and both come back that day.
